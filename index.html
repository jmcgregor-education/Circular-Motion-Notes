The swinging bucket

Fill a bucket with water and swing it in a big vertical circle. At the top, the bucket is upside down, yet the water stays in. If you swing too slowly, you get soaked.

2

Velocity in a circle

Uniform circular motion is motion in a circle with a constant radius at a constant speed.

At every instant, the object's velocity points tangent to the circle. A tangent line touches the circle at one point and is perpendicular to the radius there. This velocity is called the tangential velocity, vt.

If the string breaks, nothing pulls the ball toward the center anymore. It flies off in a straight line along the tangent, because of inertia.

3

Accelerating at constant speed

Velocity has a size and a direction. An object accelerates when its speed changes or its direction changes.

In uniform circular motion the speed stays the same, but the direction changes every instant. So the object is always accelerating. This is centripetal acceleration, ac. "Centripetal" means center seeking. It always points toward the center of the circle, perpendicular to the velocity.

Watch out: constant speed does not mean zero acceleration. A change in direction is a change in velocity.

4

Centripetal force

Newton's second law says the net force points the same way as the acceleration. So an object moving in a circle needs a net force toward the center. That net force is called the centripetal force, Fc.

Centripetal force is not a new kind of force. It is a job, and a real force has to do it.

SituationForce that does the job
Ball swung on a stringTension in the string
Car turning on a flat roadFriction between tires and road
Moon orbiting EarthGravity

The Moon is always falling toward Earth. It also moves sideways fast enough that it keeps missing. You will calculate gravity's pull in the next lesson.

Watch out: "centrifugal force" is not a real force. When a car turns left, you feel pushed right. Nothing pushes you. Your body keeps going straight because of inertia while the car turns under you. The feeling is always opposite the net force.

The equations

The period, T, is the time for one full trip around the circle. In one trip the object travels one circumference, 2πr.

The equation chain

The three equations link together. Each one takes you one step along the chain.

T, r
vt = 2πr / T
vt
ac = vt² / r

Fc = m ac
Fc

How to pick the equation

  • Find your unknown on the chain.
  • Find what you were given on the chain.
  • Walk from the given to the unknown. Use one equation for each step.

Worked example: a car on a curve

A 1200 kg car drives around a flat curve with a radius of 50 m at 15 m/s. How much friction force does the road need to supply?

Draw it
Top view: the car on a circle, r = 50 m to the center, v = 15 m/s along the tangent, and Fc = ? pointing to the center.
Name it
Friction is the centripetal force. I want Fc. I was given vt, r, and m. On the chain, vt is two steps from Fc: vt → ac → Fc.
Solve it
ac = (15 m/s)² / 50 m = 4.5 m/s²
Fc = (1200 kg)(4.5 m/s²) = 5400 N
Check it
Newtons, so the unit is right. The car weighs about 12,000 N, so 5400 N of friction is a believable amount for a dry road.

If the road can't supply 5400 N of friction (ice, for example), the car slides off in a straight line.

Watch out: if you were given T, you have to start at the very beginning of the chain and find vt first.
6

The bucket, revisited

At the top of the swing, gravity points down, which is toward the center. Swing slowly and gravity is more than enough to pull the water down. It falls out.

The slowest speed that works is when gravity alone supplies exactly the centripetal force:

mg = m v2r   so   vmin = √(gr)

The mass cancels, so the amount of water does not matter.

Example

A bucket swings in a circle with r = 0.80 m.
vmin = √((9.8)(0.80)) = √7.84 = 2.8 m/s

Any faster and the bucket pushes on the water too, so it stays in. Any slower and the water falls out of the circle.

Watch out: the water is not pushed up by a centrifugal force. It is falling the whole time. The bucket is just moving fast enough to stay underneath it.
